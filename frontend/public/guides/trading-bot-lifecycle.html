The flow, step by step

Five moments in a bot's life. The left column says when each one happens.

Onceyou click Create bot

1 · The bot is born

  1. The form turns your percentages into fractions: 4% → 0.04.
  2. The browser sends POST /bots; nginx forwards it to trading-service.
  3. Validation: symbol looks like a ticker, the strategy is one of the 11, stop-loss ≤ 50%, position size ≤ 100%, capital ≥ $100, and so on. Anything wrong → a clear error in the form.
  4. Broker check: is that broker set up? A live broker also needs the “real money” tick. A real account allows only one bot per symbol.
  5. Price check: the broker fetches a live quote. No price = unknown symbol = rejected. The price is saved as the buy & hold baseline.
  6. The bot is saved: status active, cash = your capital, 0 shares, peak equity = your capital.
  7. An audit event “Created …” is written, and the browser opens the bot's page.

Nothing is bought yet. A new bot waits for its first decision time.

code: main.py → create_bot()
Every 30 sall day, every day

2 · The scheduler checks in

For each bot that isn't archived, up to 4 at a time:

  1. Lock the bot so a button click and the scheduler can't act at the same moment. Busy? Skip it until the next tick.
  2. Finish pending orders: any order still waiting at the broker gets looked up and booked. That includes a stop order the broker has just filled.
  3. Protect (Alpaca): is a stop-loss order resting at the broker for the open position? If not, place one.
  4. Market closed? Stop here. Nothing trades outside 9:30–16:00 ET, on weekends, or on holidays.
  5. Reconcile (real brokers only): does the account really hold the shares the bot thinks it holds? If not → paused.
  6. Every 5 min: watch. Get a fresh price, update today's equity point, check the drawdown breaker, check stop-loss / take-profit (see step 5).
  7. Decision due? If yes → step 3.
code: scheduler.py → tick() → process_bot()
15:30 ETand/or 10:00 ET
every N trading days
(close: 12:30 on half days)

3 · The bot asks: buy, sell or hold?

A decision is due only when all of these are true: it's a trading day · it's inside one of the bot's Check at windows (after the open 10:00–10:30, before the close 15:30–16:00, or both) · the bot is active · it hasn't already decided for that window · at least N trading days have passed since its last decision day (a bot on both decides twice on the same day).

  1. trading-service calls decision-service /signal with the symbol, today's date, the bot's strategy, and its stop / target %.
  2. Prices: ~14 months of daily bars (open, high, low, close, volume) → the indicators the bot's strategy reads (e.g. SMA20, SMA50, RSI14 for the simple one). During the session today's bar is still forming, so its volume is scaled up to a full-day estimate.
  3. News: last 7 days of headlines, published before the decision moment (10:00 or 15:30). Recent ones count most. An AI labels the mood bullish / bearish / neutral, and headlines under 48 h old about a real event (earnings, upgrade, deal…) are flagged as catalysts.
  4. Decide: the bot's strategy gives BUY / SELL / HOLD; a textbook setup scores 0.60, each extra confirmation (volume, candle pattern, trend) adds 0.05–0.10. News that agrees adds +0.15; news that disagrees subtracts 0.15 (the news-catalyst strategy uses news in its rule instead).
  5. Explain: the AI writes 2 sentences of reasoning: which rule fired and the deciding numbers. It never changes the decision.
  6. trading-service saves a Decision row (signal, confidence, news mood, reasoning, every step) and marks today as decided.
  7. It applies the trading rule (see Rules) and writes what it did in “What the bot did”.

If decision-service fails, the bot records a HOLD with the error, trades nothing, and retries 5 min later. Today isn't marked as decided until a call succeeds.

code: trader.py → evaluate() → _act() · decision-service agent.py · strategies.py
Seconds lateronly if the rule says trade

4 · Placing the order BUY

  1. Fresh price: a quote older than 20 min is refused. No trading on stale data.
  2. Budget = cash × position size (a real account also caps it at the broker's buying power).
  3. Shares = budget ÷ (price × (1 + slippage) × (1 + fee)), rounded down to whole shares.
  4. Save the order first as new with a unique client order id. Only then send it to the broker.
  5. The broker fills it. On paper: at price + slippage, instantly. On Alpaca: usually within seconds.
  6. Book the fill: cash goes down, shares go up, entry price and cost basis are set.
  7. Stop-loss = fill × (1 − stop %). Take-profit = fill × (1 + target %). They're based on the actual fill price.
  8. Alpaca: a real stop order for all the shares goes to the broker at the stop-loss price, good till canceled. The bot page shows held at broker.
  9. Equity point updated; audit event “BUY 29 AAPL @ $337.74”.

Saving before sending means an order can't be lost or sent twice. If the network drops mid-order, the scheduler asks the broker “did you get order X?” instead of guessing.

code: trader.py → submit_order() → apply_broker_state() → _book_fill()
Every 5 minwhile holding, in market hours

5 · Holding, then selling SELL

The position is closed, always all shares, by whichever comes first:

  • Stop-loss hit: price ≤ stop → sell, reason stop-loss. Works even when the bot is paused. On Alpaca the broker's stop order fires by itself, even while this app or your computer is off, and the next check books the sale. On paper the 5-minute check sells.
  • Take-profit hit: price ≥ target → sell, reason target. Also works when paused.
  • SELL signal at a decision time with confidence ≥ minimum → reason signal.
  • You press Close position → reason manual.

Every other sell (target, signal, Close position) cancels the stop order first and only sells once the broker confirms it. Two sells could otherwise both fill and leave a short position.

On the fill: P&L = sale money − fee − cost basis. It's added to realized P&L, and the bot is flat again, waiting for the next BUY.

Drawdown breaker: if equity falls more than 20% (your setting) below its best level, the bot pauses itself. It does not panic-sell; the stop-loss still protects the position.

code: trader.py → watch() · protect() · release_stop() · close_position()

Identical to the backtest engine, so the live bot behaves like the backtest you trusted. One position at a time, long only, whole shares.

Where confidence comes from (the simple SMA/RSI strategy)

TrendRSI14SignalBaseNews agreesNews disagreesNo / neutral news
SMA20 > SMA50 (up)< 70BUY0.600.750.450.60
SMA20 ≤ SMA50 (down)> 30SELL0.600.750.450.60

Why a bot sits still: with minimum confidence 0.65, only a signal backed by agreeing news (0.75) trades. With no news, 0.60 never clears the bar. That's working as designed.

Every other strategy uses the same scale: a textbook setup is 0.60, extra confirmations add up to +0.25, news moves it ±0.15, HOLD is 0.40. Each one's exact rules, with a chart, are in the Trading Strategies guide and under the Strategy picker.

Statuses you'll see

Bot status

  • active decides on schedule, buys and sells.
  • paused no new decisions or buys. Stop-loss and take-profit still work. Paused by you, Halt all, the drawdown breaker, or a reconcile mismatch.
  • archived retired for good (only when flat). Hidden, never deleted.

Order status

new→submitted→filled
  • new saved, being sent
  • submitted broker has it, not filled yet
  • partially_filled finished with only some shares
  • canceled / rejected nothing bought or sold
  • failed never reached the broker (checked after 2 min)

Decision type

  • scheduled a decision at the bot's Check at time (10:00 and/or 15:30)
  • manual you pressed Run now while the market was open. It can trade, and it counts as today's next scheduled check.
  • preview Run now while closed or paused: shows what it would do, sends nothing, doesn't use up today's decision

Order reason

signalstop-losstargetmanual

Glossary

Every word on the bot pages, in plain language.

Money & results

Capital (allocated cash)
The money you give this bot. It never spends more. Fixed for the bot's life.
Cash
Money the bot isn't using right now.After the AAPL buy: $205.57
Equity
What the bot is worth now: cash + shares × current price.
Return
Equity vs. starting capital, in %.
Buy & hold (B&H)
What you'd have if you simply bought on day one and waited. The bar the bot has to beat.
Benchmark price
The price when the bot was created. The starting point for buy & hold.
Realized P&L
Profit or loss from trades that are finished (sold).
Unrealized P&L
Profit or loss “on paper” for shares you still hold. Changes with the price.
Win rate
Share of finished trades that made money.
Peak equity
The bot's best equity so far. Used by the drawdown breaker.
Drawdown
How far equity has fallen from its peak, in %.

Position

Position
The shares the bot holds right now. “Flat” = none.
Long
Owning shares, hoping the price goes up. The only kind of position these bots take.
Entry price
The price the shares were actually bought at (the fill).
Cost basis
Everything it cost to open the position: shares × fill price + fee.29 × $337.7388 = $9,794.43 (the screen rounds the fill to $337.74)
Stop-loss
A price below entry where the bot sells to cap the loss.entry × (1 − stop %)
Take-profit (target)
A price above entry where the bot sells to lock in the gain.entry × (1 + target %)
Reward : risk
Possible gain ÷ possible loss. 8% target vs 4% stop = 2 : 1.

Bot settings

Strategy
The rule that decides BUY / SELL / HOLD: the simple SMA/RSI trend rule (the default), or one of ten classics (trend following, momentum, breakout, mean reversion, range, pullback, reversal, gap and go, news catalyst, Fibonacci). News sentiment nudges every one of them.Details: the Trading Strategies guide
Min confidence
Signals weaker than this are ignored. Higher = fewer, pickier trades.
Every N trading days
How often the bot decides. 1 = daily, 5 ≈ weekly. Weekends and holidays don't count.Setup strategies (breakout, dips, gaps, news) suggest 1: a weekly check misses most setups
Position size
Share of the bot's cash spent on each BUY. 100% = all in.
Drawdown breaker
Auto-pause when equity falls this % below its peak. 0 = off.
Slippage
Paying a bit more when buying and getting a bit less when selling than the quoted price. Paper broker: simulated. Real brokers: a cash buffer kept aside when sizing.
Fee
Commission per trade. Only simulated by the paper broker (Alpaca stocks are commission-free).

Signals & analysis

Signal
decision-service's answer: BUY, SELL or HOLD.
Confidence
How sure the signal is, from 0 to 1.
SMA20 / SMA50
Average closing price of the last 20 / 50 days. SMA20 above SMA50 = uptrend.
RSI14
Momentum score from 0 to 100. Above 70 = overbought (rose too fast), below 30 = oversold.
Sentiment (News)
The AI's read of recent headlines: bullish (good), bearish (bad), or neutral.
Catalyst
A headline under 48 h old about a real company event: earnings, guidance, analyst up/downgrade, deal, FDA decision, recall, lawsuit. Shown as “Fresh catalysts” in a decision's steps.
RAG
“Retrieval-augmented generation”: find the most relevant stored news first, then let the AI read only that.
Reasoning / steps
The agent's explanation plus its step-by-step trail: strategy, indicator values, news, the rule that fired. Expand a decision row to see them.
What the bot did (outcome)
What happened after the signal: filled, skipped and why, or error.

Market & time

ET
New York time, the exchange's clock. Your clock (UTC+4) is 8 h ahead while the US is on summer time, 9 h in winter.15:30 ET = 23:30 or 00:30 your time
Market hours
9:30–16:00 ET, Monday–Friday, except holidays. Orders only go out in these hours.
Trading day (session)
A day the exchange is open. Weekends and holidays aren't trading days.
Half day (early close)
Days the market closes at 13:00 (e.g. the day after Thanksgiving). Bots then decide at 12:30.
Decision time (Check at)
Per bot: before the close (15:30 ET), after the open (10:00 ET), or both. Backtests can test all three; the 10:00 check is replayed from 30-minute prices.
Quote
The latest traded price and when it happened.
Stale quote
A price older than 20 min. The bot refuses to trade on it.

Orders & brokers

Broker
The company that actually buys and sells for you.
paper
Built-in simulator: real live prices, pretend money, instant fills. No account needed.
alpaca-paper
Alpaca's practice account: real order handling, pretend money. Needs paper keys.
alpaca-live
Real money. Locked until you set ALLOW_LIVE_TRADING=true, add live keys, and tick the confirmation.
Market order
“Buy/sell now at whatever the price is.” Every buy and sell the bot decides on.
Stop order
Waits at the broker and becomes a market sell once the price trades at or below its level. Alpaca bots keep one resting for the stop-loss, so it works while this app is off.
Fill
The moment an order actually happens, and the price it happened at.
Partial fill
Only some of the shares got bought or sold.
Pending order
Sent but not filled yet. While one is pending, the bot places no other orders. A resting stop order doesn't count.
Client order id
Our own unique label for each order, e.g. bot1-cf01dd…. Lets us ask the broker about it and prevents duplicates.
Buying power
How much a real account can spend right now. A bot's budget never exceeds it.

Safety & system

Scheduler
The background loop in trading-service that runs every bot.
Tick
One pass of the scheduler, every 30 s. “Scheduler running” = ticks are arriving.
Watch
The 5-minute check: new price, equity point, breaker, take-profit, and the stop-loss when the broker isn't holding it.
Lock
Makes sure only one thing works on a bot at a time. A click during a decision gets “bot is busy”.
Write-ahead order
Saving an order before sending it, so a crash can't lose or duplicate it.
Reconcile
Comparing the real account's shares with the bot's records. A mismatch pauses the bot.
Kill switch (Halt all)
One button that pauses every bot.
Equity snapshot
One chart point per bot per trading day, updated during the day.
Audit log (events)
Diary of everything: created, paused, settings changed, orders, errors.
Archive
Retire a bot without deleting its history.
