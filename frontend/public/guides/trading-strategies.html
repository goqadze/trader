The 11 trading strategies

Every backtest and every bot runs exactly one of these. Each reads the daily chart, answers BUY, SELL or HOLD, and lets the news nudge how sure it is. Here's what each one looks for, when it trades, how it exits, and how it did on past prices. Pick one under Strategy on the Backtest page or in a bot's settings.

How every strategy decides

All eleven follow the same five steps. Only step 2 and 3 differ: what the strategy looks at, and its rule. The AI never makes the call; it reads the news and writes the explanation.

1 · Daily bars~14 monthsOpen, high, low, close and volume of every trading day up to the decision.
→
2 · Factsanalyze()The indicators this strategy needs: averages, RSI, bands, levels, candle patterns.
→
3 · The ruledecide()BUY, SELL or HOLD, with a confidence: 0.60 for a textbook setup, more with confirmations.
→
4 · News tiltRAGNews that agrees adds 0.15, news that disagrees takes 0.15 away.
→
5 · The bot actsmin confidenceTrades only if confidence ≥ its minimum. Long only, one position.

One confidence scale for all

WhatConfidence
A textbook setup (the default minimum, so it trades)0.60
Each extra confirmation: volume, candle pattern, trend+0.05 / +0.10
Most a rule can give on its own0.85
News agrees / disagrees±0.15
HOLD0.40

So a bot's min confidence means the same whichever strategy it runs. Raise it to 0.65 and only setups with a confirmation or agreeing news trade.

What the news (RAG) adds

  • Mood: the AI reads the 8 most relevant headlines from the last 7 days (before the decision moment) and labels them bullish, bearish or neutral. That tilts every strategy ±0.15.
  • Fresh catalysts: headlines under 48 hours old about a real event: earnings, guidance, analyst up/downgrades, deals, FDA decisions, recalls, lawsuits, probes, buybacks. Gap and go and News catalyst use them directly.
  • News catalyst is the one strategy where news is the rule, so it gets no extra tilt.

Every signal is about your long position. SELL means “get out”. The bot only acts on it while it holds shares; it never sells short.

SMA trend + RSI

defaulttrend

The original, simple rule. Is the short-term average price above the long-term one? Then the stock is trending up, so be in. RSI stops it from buying right after a sprint.

BUYSMA20 above SMA50, and RSI14 below 70 (not overbought).
SELLSMA20 at or below SMA50, and RSI14 above 30 (not already oversold).
Confidence
Always 0.60 (no bonuses); news ±0.15. Extreme RSI → HOLD.
Suggested
every 5 days · stop 4% · target 8%
Best for
Steady trends. The baseline every other strategy has to beat.
Watch out
In sideways markets the averages cross back and forth: many small losses (“whipsaw”).

01

Trend following

trendgolden cross + ADX

Don't predict, follow. When the 50-day average crosses above the 200-day (the “golden cross”) and ADX says the trend is strong, ride it for months. Leave only when the trend itself breaks.

BUYSMA50 above SMA200 · price above SMA50 · ADX ≥ 20 with buyers in control (+DI above −DI).
SELLDeath cross (SMA50 below SMA200), or price below SMA200 with sellers in control.
Confidence
0.60 · +0.10 if ADX ≥ 25 · +0.05 if SMA200 is rising
Suggested
every 5 days · stop 8% · target 100% (effectively none: the death cross is the exit)
Best for
Strong multi-month trends. Needs ~11 months of history before its first decision.
Watch out
Late in and late out: it gives back a chunk at the end of every trend. Few trades, low win rate, big winners.

+26.1% avg · 25 trades · 36% won · worst drop −23%
02

Momentum

momentumMACD + RSI

Stocks that have been rising tend to keep rising for a while. Buy strength that is still building, not strength that is already exhausted.

BUY3-month return above zero · price above SMA50 · MACD above its signal line and the gap widening · RSI between 50 and 70.
SELLMACD below its signal with RSI under 50 (momentum faded), or price below SMA50 with MACD below zero.
Confidence
0.60 · +0.10 MACD above zero · +0.10 volume ≥ 1.5× average
Suggested
every 5 days · stop 6% · target 15%
Best for
Market leaders in trending markets.
Watch out
Skips RSI above 70 on purpose (no chasing). Quick exits can shake it out of long trends.

03

Breakout

breakout20-day high + volume

A stock stuck under a ceiling finally closes above it, with lots of buyers behind the move. Volume is the proof: without it, most breakouts fail.

BUYClose above the highest high of the 20 days before · volume ≥ 1.5× its 20-day average · close in the upper half of the day's range (not rejected).
SELLClose below the lowest low of the 10 days before (the Turtle traders' exit).
Confidence
0.60 · +0.10 tight base (the 20 days before spanned ≤ 4 normal days' moves) · +0.05 above SMA50
Suggested
every day · stop 5% · target 15% · before the close (the day's volume is complete)
Best for
Stocks leaving a tight, quiet consolidation.
Watch out
False breakouts that fall straight back. A weekly check misses the breakout day.

04

Mean reversion

mean reversionBollinger + RSI

Prices stretched far from their average tend to snap back. Buy a sharp oversold dip once it starts bouncing; sell when it's back at the average.

BUYIn the last 3 days the low went below the lower Bollinger Band with RSI under 30 · today closes back inside the bands on an up candle · not in a strong downtrend.
SELLClose at or above the middle band (the 20-day average): the snap-back is done.
Confidence
0.60 · +0.10 above SMA200 (a dip inside a long uptrend) · +0.05 hammer or bullish engulfing
Suggested
every day · stop 5% · target 8%
Best for
Range-bound stocks, and dips inside long uptrends.
Watch out
Rare. A crash looks like a dip at first, so it skips “falling knives” (ADX ≥ 30 with sellers in control).

Range trading

mean reversionsupport & resistance

When a stock bounces between a floor (support) and a ceiling (resistance), buy near the floor and sell near the ceiling. Only when the market is truly going sideways.

BUYADX under 20 (no trend) · both edges of the last 40 days' range tested at least twice · range 4–30% tall · price in the bottom quarter · up candle.
SELLPrice in the top quarter of the range, or a close below support (the range broke).
Confidence
BUY 0.60 · +0.10 hammer / bullish engulfing · +0.05 RSI under 40. SELL +0.10 with a shooting star / bearish engulfing; a broken support is 0.70.
Suggested
every day · stop 4% · target 8%
Best for
Sideways, choppy stocks.
Watch out
Every range ends with a breakout. The stop-loss is your protection.

+4.3% avg · 17 trades · 53% won · worst drop −4%
06

Pullback to the 20 EMA

trendswing trading

In an uptrend, wait for a dip back to the 20-day average and buy when buyers step in again. You join the trend at a discount instead of chasing it.

BUYEMA20 above a rising EMA50 · today's or yesterday's low touched EMA20 (within 0.5%) · close back above EMA20 on an up candle.
SELLEMA20 crosses below EMA50, or price closes below EMA50.
Confidence
0.60 · +0.10 hammer / bullish engulfing · +0.05 RSI 40–60 (a calm dip)
Suggested
every day · stop 5% · target 12%
Best for
Healthy uptrends with regular pullbacks. Holds days to weeks.
Watch out
A “pullback” can be the start of a reversal; the EMA50 exit limits the damage.

Reversal

mean reversionRSI divergence

After a long fall, the price makes a new low but RSI doesn't: sellers are running out of steam. Buy once an up candle confirms the turn.

BUYTwo recent swing lows: the newer one lower in price but higher in RSI (the first with RSI under 35, the newer at most 5 days old) · today closes up, above yesterday's close.
SELLThe mirror at the top: a higher high with a lower RSI (the first above 65), plus a down candle.
Confidence
0.60 · +0.10 hammer / bullish engulfing · +0.05 close above yesterday's high
Suggested
every day · stop 5% · target 10%
Best for
Oversold stocks after a long decline.
Watch out
Contrarian: when it's wrong, it bought a falling stock. Rare signals.

+4.8% avg · 13 trades · 62% won · worst drop −7%
08

Gap and go

eventgap trading

When big news makes a stock open far above yesterday's close and it never looks back, the move often keeps going.

BUYOpens ≥ 2% above yesterday's close (and ≥ half a normal day's move) · never falls back to yesterday's close · closes at or above the open · volume ≥ 1.5×.
SELLA gap down that stays below the open and never gets back to yesterday's close.
Confidence
0.60 · +0.10 a fresh news catalyst explains the gap · +0.05 closes in the top quarter of the day
Suggested
every day · stop 3% · target 6% · after the open (by 10:00 you can see if the gap holds)
Best for
Earnings and news days.
Watch out
Gaps that “fill” (fall back). Backtest it with Check at “After the open” (the last 60 days): a close decision sees the whole day, which is a different rule.

+0.5% avg · 17 trades · 41% won · worst drop −4%
09

News catalyst

eventRAG-driven

Trade the news, but only when the market agrees with it. Earnings surprises especially tend to keep drifting in their direction for days (“post-earnings drift”).

BUYNews sentiment bullish and the price is up today.
SELLNews sentiment bearish and the price is down today.
Confidence
0.55 base (an opinion piece on a quiet day stays under the default 0.60) · +0.10 a fresh catalyst · +0.10 volume ≥ 1.5×. No extra news tilt.
Suggested
every day · stop 4% · target 10% · both 10:00 and 15:30
Best for
Event-driven stocks. Needs working news keys.
Watch out
Only as good as the news feed. Not in the price-only replay, because it needs past news.

Right now all three news sources (Alpaca, Polygon, Finnhub) answer 401, so only news already stored is used. Fix the keys in decision-service/.env before running this one.

10

Fibonacci retracement

trendgolden zone

After a strong move up, prices often pull back about half to two thirds of it before continuing. Buy the bounce in that “golden zone”.

BUYA clean up-move of 8%+ in the last 60 days (its high at least 3 days ago) · today's low reaches the 50% level · the close holds above 61.8% on an up candle · 78.6% never broken.
SELLClose below the 78.6% level: the move failed.
Confidence
0.60 · +0.10 hammer / bullish engulfing · +0.05 above SMA200
Suggested
every day · stop 5% · target 12%
Best for
Trending stocks after a sharp run-up.
Watch out
The Fibonacci ratios are a popular convention, not a law. Treat it as a disciplined “buy the pullback” rule.

No strategy beat simply holding the stock, on any of the ten. 2024–25 was a strong bull market (NVDA alone rose 287%), and timing rules spend part of the time in cash, which costs a lot when prices only go up.

Why these ten

They were picked from four strategy round-ups (Evest, DataDrivenInvestor, XBTFX, DBS) for what these bots can really run: US stocks, daily bars, one symbol per bot, long only.

Scalping, high-frequency and day tradingNeed minute-by-minute data and fast execution; the bots decide on daily bars.

Indicators A–Z

Every word the strategies and their decision steps use, in plain language.

Trend

SMA
Simple moving average: the plain average close of the last N days. Smooths out the noise.SMA50 = average of the last 50 closes
EMA
Exponential moving average: like an SMA, but recent days count more, so it turns sooner.
Golden cross / death cross
SMA50 crossing above SMA200 (golden: a long uptrend may be starting) or below it (death: it may be over).
ADX
Average Directional Index, 0–100: how strong a trend is, not which way. Under 20 = no trend (sideways), over 25 = a real trend.
+DI / −DI
The two halves of ADX: +DI measures the push up, −DI the push down. Whichever is higher is in control.
Uptrend / downtrend
Higher highs and higher lows (up), or lower highs and lower lows (down). The strategies read it from averages.

Momentum

RSI
Relative Strength Index, 0–100: recent gains vs losses. Over 70 = overbought (rose fast), under 30 = oversold. The new strategies use Wilder's version, as charting apps do.
MACD
Moving Average Convergence Divergence: the 12-day EMA minus the 26-day EMA. Above zero = short-term prices above longer-term ones.
MACD signal line
A 9-day EMA of the MACD. MACD crossing above it = momentum turning up.
MACD histogram
MACD minus its signal line. Growing bars = momentum speeding up.
3-month return
How much the price changed over the last 63 trading days. Momentum only buys stocks that are up.
Divergence
Price and RSI disagreeing: a lower low in price with a higher low in RSI (bullish), or the reverse at a top (bearish). A sign the move is tiring.

Volatility & levels

Bollinger Bands
A band 2 standard deviations above and below the 20-day average. About 95% of closes land inside; outside = stretched.
Middle band
The 20-day average inside the Bollinger Bands: “the mean” that mean reversion expects prices to return to.
ATR
Average True Range: how far the stock usually moves in a day, gaps included. Used to judge whether a gap or a range is big for this stock.
Support
A price floor where buyers kept stepping in before.
Resistance
A price ceiling where sellers kept showing up before.
20-day high / 10-day low
The highest high of the last 20 days and lowest low of the last 10 (Donchian channels, used by the famous Turtle traders).
Base (consolidation)
A quiet stretch where the price moves sideways in a tight range. Tight bases often lead to strong breakouts.
Fibonacci levels
Points 50%, 61.8% and 78.6% of the way back down a move, measured from its high.
Golden zone
Between the 50% and 61.8% Fibonacci levels: where pullbacks in strong moves often stop.

Volume & candles

Volume
How many shares traded that day. Heavy volume = many people agree with the move.
Relative volume
Today's volume ÷ the average of the 20 days before.1.5× = 50% busier than usual. Live checks scale a still-forming day up to a full-day estimate first.
Candlestick
One day drawn as a box from open to close (green up, red down) with wicks to the high and low.
Hammer
A small body at the top of a long lower wick: sellers pushed it down, buyers pushed it all the way back. A bullish sign after a drop.
Shooting star
The upside-down hammer: a long upper wick, small body at the bottom. Buyers got rejected. Bearish after a rise.
Bullish / bearish engulfing
A day whose body completely covers the previous day's body in the other direction. A strong turn signal.
Gap
Today opening well above (gap up) or below (gap down) yesterday's close, usually after news.
Gap fill
The price falling back to yesterday's close, erasing the gap. Gap and go skips gaps that fill.

News

Sentiment
The AI's read of recent headlines: bullish, bearish or neutral. Tilts confidence ±0.15.
Catalyst
A real, company-specific event from the last 48 hours: earnings, guidance, up/downgrade, deal, FDA decision, recall, lawsuit, buyback.
Post-earnings drift
The tendency of a stock to keep moving for days or weeks in the direction of an earnings surprise. The idea behind the news catalyst strategy.
RAG
“Retrieval-augmented generation”: find the most relevant stored headlines first, then let the AI read only those.

Decisions

Setup
The situation a strategy waits for (an oversold dip, a breakout day). Most days there is none, so the answer is HOLD.
Confirmation
Extra evidence on top of the setup (volume, a candle pattern, the bigger trend) that raises confidence.
Exit rule
The strategy's own reason to sell. The stop-loss and take-profit work on top of it.
Whipsaw
Getting in and out repeatedly for small losses as a signal flips back and forth in a sideways market.
Falling knife
A stock dropping hard that looks “cheap” but keeps falling. Mean reversion refuses to buy one.
Contrarian
Trading against the current move, betting it turns. The reversal strategy does this.

No term matches that. Try a shorter word.
